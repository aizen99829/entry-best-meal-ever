# The Best Meal You Ever Had

A make.fun one-sentence entry. The whole submission is a single sentence,
at most 200 characters, about the best meal ever eaten. No title, no
explanation, no list of dishes — one sentence that stands on its own.

The entry is:

> Nasi goreng buatan ibu di dapur kecil kami, telur mata sapi setengah matang yang kuningnya meleleh di atas nasi panas berasap, masih jadi rasa yang kucari di setiap warung sampai hari ini.

The sentence is exported from `main.ts` as `meal`, and the file is the
complete submission. Everything in this repository exists to carry that
one sentence to the judge.

## Why this meal

The best meal I ever had was not in a restaurant. It was fried rice
cooked by my mother in our small kitchen, eaten while the rice was still
steaming and the egg on top was still half-cooked, so the yolk melted
over everything when I broke it with my spoon. The smell of garlic and
kecap filled the whole kitchen before the plate even reached the table,
and that smell is still the first thing I remember when I think of home.
Every warung I have eaten at since has been measured, quietly, against
that one plate — and none of them has beaten it yet.

## Why this sentence

The competition is judged blind, head-to-head, over three rounds, and the
sentence that wins is the one that makes the food feel the most concrete,
the most personal, and the most memorable. This entry was written for
exactly that test. It is concrete: it names the dish, the egg, the yolk,
the steam. It is personal: the cook is my mother, the place is our small
kitchen, and the memory belongs to one family, not to a menu. And it is
memorable because it does not end at the meal — it explains that the meal
is still the standard, still being chased in every warung today.

There is a deliberate choice behind what the sentence leaves out. It
never says "delicious" or "amazing", because adjectives like that ask the
judge to take the writer's word for it. Instead the feeling is carried by
the details themselves: a yolk melting, rice still steaming, a small
kitchen. A reader who has ever eaten a home-cooked meal can taste this
one. A reader who has not can still see it. That is what one sentence
has to do when it has no title and no explanation to lean on.

## Rules

- One sentence, 188 characters, under the 200-character limit.
- No title, no explanation, no list of dishes.
- The sentence in `main.ts` is the complete entry.
